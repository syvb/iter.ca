I want to be better at keeping secrets: I think I'm kinda average at the skill of keeping some information secret from someone I'm talking to, and I want to get better at it. Keeping secrets has a cost to me – I have to be careful to avoid revealing it. If I can get better at keeping secrets then it should have a lower cost to me, and I'd be more willing to learn people's secrets! It seems pretty useful for me to be better at this; keeping secrets in high stakes situations seems like a useful skill to have. I want to be better at things like negotiations, where it might be useful for me to selectively reveal information.

When people tell me secrets, I usually default to a policy of "Don't make the person who told me the secret regret telling it to me". Keeping secrets in that way involves two main skills:

- correctly working out what information you should avoid revealing
- properly following through on your intentions from the first point

Hidden role games, like [Blood on the Clocktower](https://bloodontheclocktower.com), let you practice both of those skills a lot! You have to quickly work out what information you know is bad for other people to know, and make sure you don't accidentally reveal information you shouldn't. I find them a really fun way to practice keeping secrets!

Keeping some information secret from someone else is a similar skill to deceiving them. To keep a secret you have to act as if you don't know the secret – your behavior should be the same as if you didn't learn it. But I don't get a lot of chances to practice this kind of secret-keeping in real life, because I don't currently have to keep that many secrets! Hidden role games are a good way to practice the skills of revealing information selectively – if you're evil, you have to avoid revealing that secret information to other players. They also involve a lot of fast-paced discussion, so I can practice managing secrecy in a fast-paced environment. Playing hidden role games helps me avoid accidentally saying confidential information because I didn't think before speaking.

Hidden role games aren't *exactly* like keeping secrets – I'm not just glomarizing about if I'm a demon or not, I'm actively trying to trick people into having an incorrect world model. But they're a pretty close analogue, and the skills that you build in them can transfer pretty well to keeping secrets in the real world. When you're keeping some information secret from someone you're acting as if you don't know it; when you play a hidden role game (as evil), you're typically acting as if you didn't know that you're evil.

**Isn't it bad to practice deception?** I do think it's a little bad for me to practice the skill of deceiving people; I worry I might start enjoying making people have wrong world models. I just think the benefits of getting better at keeping secrets outweigh the negatives here; I haven't noticed much of a pull towards being deceptive after playing hidden role games.
