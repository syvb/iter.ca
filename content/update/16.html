research

I tried backpropagating through Qwen3-VL-8B's vision stack to the input image for fun! Instead of training the model weights, I use gradient descent to train the input in order to make it match the soft tokens that come out of Qwen3’s vision stack. At first I could only get this to work for one of the early intermediate states, but eventually I tried using the Muon optimizer instead of Adam and it worked great! (I probably could’ve gotten Adam to work too if I’d twiddled with the hyperparameters more; Muon is good but it’s not that good.) You can try it yourself on your own images if you want to play with it! (Hugging Face has a cool serverless GPU thing that I used!)

(Qwen3-VL uses a slightly non-standard vision stack where it takes the residual stream from the middle of the vision encoder and adds it to the residual stream of the LLM partway through, in addition to treating the final output as a soft token. So “deepstack8” which is part-way through the vision stack is actually used for something beyond getting to main.)

I looked into if the upwards inflection in AI capabilities progress earlier this year was because of a quirk of the Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI): specifically, the way it assumes that benchmarks have a logistic saturation curve, which would have more of an impact now that many benchmarks are being saturated. Nope - it turns out AI capabilities really are inflecting upwards, even if you change the ECI’s assumption about benchmark saturation curves it still is.
